\begin{abstract}
%% ANCRE : MESURES.md §1 (la porte), §2 (mineur), §3 (oracle).
A companion paper argued that in mechanised proof search the last mile is
located, not crossed: a search that fails should name exactly what it lacks.
This paper reports the next step --- a \emph{walker} that crosses the stretch
the previous system could only locate. Given a goal that backward chaining
alone cannot close --- measured: on a four-element reassociation over a derived
operation $a \oplus b := (a+b)+1$, chaining exhausts its budget in 692\,s ---
the walker mines the goal's own subterms for repeated patterns, rediscovers
$\oplus$ without being told it exists, conjectures laws for it from an open
list of schemas, lets a numeric oracle kill the false ones in milliseconds,
has the kernel certify the true ones, and re-attempts the goal on the
\emph{compressed} pool of derived lemmas alone. The same goal then closes,
kernel-checked, end to end in 414\,s --- less time than chaining needs to
fail. Two measured facts frame the contribution.
First, compression is a \emph{replacement}, not an addition: re-attempting on
the derived lemma alone takes 72.6\,s, while adding it to the raw laws takes
962\,s --- a factor of 13, because new knowledge enlarges the search space
unless the old knowledge is set aside. Second, the walker's own first
component failed in a way worth reporting: ranking candidate patterns by size
selected only fragments of the huge $\tau$-developments (a triple-$\oplus$
term has $\approx$13{,}700 nodes) and missed $\oplus$ entirely; the fix ---
pairing subterms by root signature --- is itself a measured design law. As
everywhere in this series, the kernel guards every step: a bad proposal costs
a dead route, never a false theorem.
\end{abstract}

%% ============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ANCRE : PLAN_ARTICLES.md (la porte, 10 aout) ; MESURES.md §1.
%% ============================================================================

The companion paper on failure reporting ended on a gate it refused to cross:
its editorial plan states that no walker should be published before it has
closed at least one goal that chaining alone cannot close. This paper begins
by crossing that gate, measured on both sides, and everything else in it is a
consequence of how the crossing was done.

\paragraph{The claim, in one paragraph.} A goal that defeats a bounded
backward search does not usually defeat it by depth alone. What is missing is
a \emph{waypoint}: an intermediate statement that, once certified and placed
in the pool, makes the remaining distance short. The walker's job is to invent
candidate waypoints from the goal's own structure, discard the false ones
cheaply, pay the kernel's price for the true ones, and then attempt the goal
from the waypoint rather than from the raw laws. None of this requires the
machine to be right: every candidate is judged --- first by a numeric oracle
that can only refute, then by the kernel that alone can certify. A wrong
proposal costs seconds; an unsound one is impossible.

\paragraph{Contributions.} All figures were measured on 21 August 2026 and
are reproducible from the repository (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The gate, crossed and asserted on both sides}
  (Sections~\ref{sec:porte} and~\ref{sec:marche}): chaining alone fails on
  $B_4$ in 692\,s; one unattended call to the walker closes it,
  kernel-checked, in 414\,s. A test in the repository asserts both sides.
\item \textbf{Replacement, not addition, measured three times}
  (Sections~\ref{sec:porte} and~\ref{sec:marche}): the derived lemma alone,
  72.6\,s; with one more true lemma, 361\,s; added to the raw laws, 962\,s;
  all six certified lemmas at once, over 580\,s and killed. Hence the
  walker's compression ladder.
\item \textbf{A miner that rediscovers the operation from the goal}
  (Section~\ref{sec:mineur}) --- including the measured failure of its first
  version, which ranked by size and saw only $\tau$-development noise.
\item \textbf{A refutation oracle with a stated blind spot}
  (Section~\ref{sec:oracle}): one added layer makes derived-operation
  conjectures visible; what stays invisible cost a measured 11\,s of failed
  certifications on this bench.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background: the bench, the budget, the gap}
\label{sec:background}
%% ANCRE : test_autonomie.py (v16-v18) ; MESURES.md de A2 ; besoin.py.
%% ============================================================================

We reuse the toy-algebra bench on which the failure-reporting paper measured
its rewriting organs. The base theory provides cardinal addition $a+b$ and its
two raw laws --- iterated associativity and commutativity --- as the only
facts in the pool. On top of it, a derived operation is defined and never
named anywhere in the repository:
\[
a \oplus b \;:=\; (a+b)+1 .
\]
The engine's budget is not a guess: the minimal raw rewriting chain for the
three-element associativity of $\oplus$ was measured at five steps, and five
is therefore the engine's cap (\texttt{max\_pas=5}); with a cap of three, that
same goal failed. All experiments below inherit this measured budget.

The goal that crosses the gate is the four-element reassociation
\[
B_4 \;:\;\; ((a \oplus b) \oplus c) \oplus d \;=\; a \oplus (b \oplus (c
\oplus d)),
\]
true, and out of reach of a five-step raw chain: each application of
$\oplus$-associativity costs about five raw steps, and $B_4$ needs two.

\section{The gate, measured on both sides}
\label{sec:porte}
%% ANCRE : MESURES.md §1 (EXP1-EXP3).
%% ============================================================================

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.42\linewidth}L{0.24\linewidth}r@{}}
\toprule
experiment & verdict & time \\
\midrule
chaining alone on $B_4$ (raw pool) & \textbf{fails}, one gap named & 692.5\,s \\
certifying the lemma $\oplus$-assoc & closed, kernel-checked & 4--7\,s \\
$B_4$ on the \emph{compressed} pool (lemma alone) & \textbf{closed}, 0 hypotheses & 72.6\,s \\
$B_4$ on the cumulative pool (raw $+$ lemma) & closed & 962.4\,s \\
false variant $F_4$ on the compressed pool & stays open (correct) & 79.1\,s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three readings, in decreasing order of importance.

\paragraph{The gate holds.} The same goal, from the same pool, under the same
budgets: chaining exhausts itself in 692\,s and returns a named gap; the
two-step walk --- certify the waypoint, then re-attempt from it --- closes it.
``Cannot close'' here is not an asymptotic claim; it is the measured behaviour
of the full 21-organ engine at its measured budget.

\paragraph{Compression is a replacement, not an addition.} The most useful
number in the table is the least intuitive one: adding the certified lemma to
the raw laws makes the search \emph{thirteen times slower} than replacing them
with it (962\,s against 72.6\,s). Every fact in the pool is a rewriting
candidate at every node; new knowledge enlarges the space unless the old
knowledge is set aside. The walker therefore re-attempts goals on the derived
lemmas \emph{alone} --- and says so in its journal, rather than silently.

\paragraph{Certification cost is noisy.} The same lemma certified twice took
3.8\,s and then 7.4\,s depending on cache state. We quote ``a few seconds''
and refuse a single figure.

\section{The walker}
\label{sec:marcheur}
%% ANCRE : autonomie/marcheur.py (docstring de module).
%% ============================================================================

The walker's state is a pair (goal, pool of certified facts). One step:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sense.} Run the need organ. Closed: done. Open: a list of named
  gaps --- the failure object of the companion paper.
\item \textbf{Mine.} Extract repeated term patterns from the goal itself, by
  anti-unification with shared slots, ranked by the MDL gain
  $(\mathrm{occurrences}-1) \times \mathrm{size}$ --- the compression
  criterion of the notion-mining organ, transposed from formulas to terms.
\item \textbf{Conjecture.} Instantiate law schemas on each binary pattern:
  commutativity, associativity, idempotence. The schema list is \emph{open};
  nothing proves these three suffice, and the paper does not claim it.
\item \textbf{Refute cheaply.} A numeric oracle looks for small
  counterexamples. A false conjecture dies in milliseconds, never in minutes
  of kernel time. The oracle can only refute: no counterexample authorises
  spending, proves nothing.
\item \textbf{Certify, compress, retry.} Surviving conjectures go to the need
  organ over the raw pool; the kernel judges. Certified lemmas form the
  compressed pool on which the goal is re-attempted. No progress in a round:
  stop, and return the terminal gaps --- the walker, like the organ it wraps,
  fails by naming.
\end{enumerate}

The safety principle is unchanged since the first proposer organ: the walker
\emph{suggests}, the kernel \emph{judges}. No theorem object is built in the
walker's code; everything that enters the pool came out of the need organ,
hence out of the kernel.

\section{The miner, including its first failure}
\label{sec:mineur}
%% ANCRE : MESURES.md §2 ; marcheur.py (miner_motifs, docstring).
%% ============================================================================

The miner answers the question the reader should be asking: \emph{who told
the machine about $\oplus$?} Nobody. The pattern is recovered from the goal.

\paragraph{Version 1 failed, and the failure is a design law.} The first
miner collected the goal's subterms and anti-unified the 24 largest. It found
nothing usable: in this kernel, terms are opaque $\tau$-developments, and a
triple-$\oplus$ term has ${\approx}13{,}700$ nodes, so the largest subterms
are all \emph{fragments of the developments' insides} --- and the small
instances $a \oplus b$, the ones that carry the pattern, never entered the
top-24. The top pattern was a three-slot context of MDL gain 13{,}737,
useless for conjecturing, and $\oplus$ was absent from the list. Ranking by
size selects noise.

\paragraph{Version 2 pairs by root signature.} Subterms are grouped by root
signature (tag, name, binder, arity) and each is anti-unified with its
size-neighbours \emph{within its group}: $O(n)$ pairs instead of $O(n^2)$,
and two instances of the same operation necessarily meet. Occurrences are
then counted over \emph{all} subterms, and candidates are validated by
reconstruction: a pattern applied to the pieces of its own generator must
rebuild it identically --- assemblages are hashable, so this is one $O(1)$
equality, never a structural guess. On $B_4$ the head pattern comes out with
6 occurrences and gain 4{,}045, and applying it to $(a,b)$ yields exactly
$\oplus(a,b)$: mining took 1.1\,s.

%% ============================================================================
\section{The oracle had a hole, and one layer was enough}
\label{sec:oracle}
%% ANCRE : MESURES.md §3 ; oracle_num.py (table, docstring).
%% ============================================================================

The refutation step exposed a measured blind spot. The oracle evaluates terms
by a reconstruction table --- numerals, their sums, their products --- because
terms here are opaque: there is nothing to descend into. But --- writing
$\mathrm{succ}$ for the successor operation, the $+1$ of the bench's
$\oplus$ --- $\mathrm{succ}(\text{sum})$ was in no entry, so \emph{every} conjecture about
a derived operation evaluated to ``unknown'': invisible, neither refutable
nor authorised.

One layer fixed it: for every term already in the table with value $v$, add
its successor with value $v+1$. After the extension, the idempotence schema
for $\oplus$ --- false --- dies in under a millisecond, before any kernel
call; commutativity and associativity survive the sweep (about a second each)
and proceed to certification. The limit is stated where the code is: one
layer only. A nested derived term such as $\mathrm{succ}(\mathrm{succ}(a+b)+c)$
is still outside the table, the full closure would cost $|\text{table}|^2$,
and on such conjectures the oracle stays silent --- it never affirms.

\section{The walk, end to end}
\label{sec:marche}
%% ANCRE : MESURES.md §4 (exp4_b4.out, sonde horodatee, 21 aout).
%% ============================================================================

Everything above described organs; this section reports the machine running
them unattended. One call --- \texttt{marcher($B_4$, raw pool)} --- with a
timestamped probe on its journal:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.58\linewidth}rr@{}}
\toprule
phase & window & duration \\
\midrule
mining (4 patterns, gains 4045/1771/1155/895) & 0--4.0\,s & 4.0\,s \\
12 conjectures: 6 certified, 2 refuted, 4 failed & 4.0--53.2\,s & 49.2\,s \\
\quad $\oplus$-commutativity certified & & 20.2\,s \\
\quad $\oplus$-associativity certified & & 16.8\,s \\
\quad idempotence refuted (at $x=0$, resp.\ $x=1$) & & ${<}0.1$\,s \\
retry, ladder rung 1 (the head pattern's 2 lemmas) & 53.2--414.2\,s & 361.0\,s \\
\midrule
\textbf{closed} --- kernel-checked, 0 hypotheses, 22 axioms & & \textbf{414.2\,s} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The walk closes in 414\,s a goal that chaining spends 692\,s \emph{failing}
to close. Figure~\ref{fig:chronologie} puts the four searches of this paper
side by side. But the number worth the section is how the retry got its
shape.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering

\includegraphics{chronologie.png}}
\caption{Four searches on the same goal $B_4$, same budgets, one machine
(script: \texttt{scripts/gen\_chronologie.py}, figures from
\texttt{MESURES.md}). Chaining alone exhausts its budget and fails; the walk

\label{fig:chronologie}

\paragraph{The walk first failed by its own richness.} The first version
re-attempted the goal on \emph{all} certified lemmas at once --- six of them,
since the miner's lesser patterns also yield certifiable laws. That retry
exceeded 580\,s and was killed: the walker had grown its pool into its own
obstacle, exactly the failing-by-growing signature the companion paper
documents for raw search. The fix is the \emph{compression ladder}: re-attempt
on the head pattern's lemmas alone, widen to the next pattern only on
failure. Rung 1 sufficed. And a finer measurement sharpens the replacement
law: the rung's two lemmas ($\oplus$-comm $+$ $\oplus$-assoc) close $B_4$ in
361\,s, while $\oplus$-assoc \emph{alone} closes it in 72.6\,s ---
even one true, on-topic, certified lemma too many costs a factor of five.

\paragraph{The price of the oracle's blind spot, measured.} Four conjectures
(associativity and idempotence of the two lesser patterns) were out of the
one-layer oracle's fragment, went to certification, and failed there ---
about 11\,s of the 414. That is what the stated limit of
Section~\ref{sec:oracle} costs on this bench: real, small, and on the
journal rather than hidden.

\paragraph{The guard, and what it cost to run.} The same walk was pointed at
a false variant $F_4$ (a $d$ where a $c$ should be). It certified the same
lemmas --- the lemmas are about $\oplus$, not about the goal --- and then
failed the first ladder rung honestly: 788\,s of exhaustion, one terminal gap
named, nothing closed. The kernel makes the strong half of this guarantee
trivial (no false theorem is \emph{possible}); what the run adds is the weak
half --- the walker does not loop forever and does not claim.

Then the honest caveat. On the higher rungs (pools of four to six lemmas),
the guard run died three times --- silently: no exit code, no traceback, no
system event; cause not identified, correlated with pool size, not duration.
The repository's guard test therefore caps the ladder at one rung
\emph{and asserts that the journal says so} --- the cap is a stated
workaround, not a hidden optimisation. A failing walk that must climb the
whole ladder is expensive by construction; how expensive, we cannot yet
measure to the end.

\section{What the walk does not find}
\label{sec:limites-goldbach}
%% ANCRE : P6 — A MESURER (marcheur pointe sur un but Goldbach).
%% ============================================================================

Pointed at the general Goldbach statement --- the $\tau$-formalised goal of
the companion paper on mapping the open, with the same two raw laws in the
pool --- the walker did everything this paper describes. It mined four
patterns from the statement (the head one, 8 occurrences, is the sum that
$2n = p+q$ is made of); it \emph{certified} commutativity and associativity
lemmas --- true, kernel-checked, and about the \emph{addition} in the
statement, not about primality; it refuted two idempotence schemas at $x=1$
and $x=2$; it re-attempted on its ladder; and it ended \textbf{terminal in
35.7\,s}, one gap named, nothing closed, 22 axioms as always.

This is the paper's negative claim, and its boundary. The walk creates no
mathematical information: certified true lemmas about the operations a
conjecture mentions do not move the conjecture. The walker crosses last
miles that are \emph{pavable} --- closable with lemmas of the theory it
already holds; on an open conjecture it does the only honest thing it knows,
which is to stop quickly and say where it stopped. What the machine can say
\emph{about} that gap --- reformulating it as properties of a $\tau$-term
--- is the companion paper's subject, not this one's.

Every figure in this paper was measured on 21 August 2026 and recorded, with
its experiment, in \texttt{article/marcheur/MESURES.md}; the experiment

(\texttt{exp1\_porte\_directe.py}, \texttt{exp3\_pool\_comprime.py},

\texttt{outils\_ia/decouvertes/autonomie/test\_general.py}: a fast one

slow ones asserting both sides of the gate and the false-variant guard.
Durations are whole-call wall-clock on one machine (Python 3.13, Windows);
the certification variance of Section~\ref{sec:porte} applies throughout.

The verdicts, read off the last line of the output files, never off a
notification: the fast miner test, 	extbf{1 passed in 34.0\,s}; the two
slow tests --- both sides of the gate, and the capped guard ---
	extbf{2 passed in 2673.1\,s} (44\,min\,33).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ANCRE : ../references.bib — UNIQUEMENT des entrees deja verifiees a la
%% source (regle 0-entree-(v?), campagne du 2 aout).
%% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Theory exploration.} The closest relative of the
mine--conjecture--refute--certify loop is theory exploration as practised by
Hipster in Isabelle/HOL~\cite{johansson2014hipster}, in the QuickSpec lineage:
enumerate candidate lemmas about given operations, filter them by testing,
prove the survivors. Two differences matter here. Hipster explores
\emph{around a theory} --- its input is a set of operations, its output a
library; the walker is \emph{goal-directed} --- its input is one goal it must
close, the operations are not given but mined from that goal's own subterms,
and a conjecture is only certified because a crossing needs it. And our
setting is a Bourbaki $\tau$-calculus on an LCF kernel, where terms are
opaque developments: the mining step (and its measured failure) has no
counterpart when terms are readable trees. The survey of automated
conjecturing~\cite{conjecturingsurvey2026} maps the wider landscape.

\paragraph{Counterexample before proof.} Killing conjectures with small
models before attempting proof is established Isabelle practice ---
Nitpick and its integration with Sledgehammer~\cite{blanchette2011nitpick};
recent work learns to generate formal counterexamples with language
models~\cite{learningtodisprove2026}. Our oracle is deliberately humbler: a
reconstruction table over numerals, extended by one measured layer, whose
blind spots are stated rather than smoothed over --- and whose verdicts are
asymmetric by construction, refutation certain, silence otherwise.

\paragraph{Library learning and compression.} The MDL criterion the miner
ranks by --- name what repeats, weighted by what naming saves --- is the
compression principle of wake-sleep library learning in
DreamCoder~\cite{ellis2021dreamcoder}. What the bench adds is a caution
those systems rarely measure: a learned abstraction only pays if it
\emph{replaces} the primitives in the search space; kept alongside them, it
made our search thirteen times slower.

\paragraph{Learned proposers and guided search.} GPT-$f$~\cite{polu2020gptf},
HyperTree Proof Search~\cite{lample2022htps} and
ENIGMA~\cite{jakubuv2020enigma} learn to propose or rank inference steps,
with success measured over benchmark corpora. The walker shares their
architecture-level premise --- an unsound proposer behind an exact judge ---
but not their mechanism: it learns nothing, retains nothing between runs,
and proposes from the structure of the one goal in front of it. The
comparison it invites is therefore not proof rates but failure behaviour:
what remains when the run does not close.

\paragraph{Conjectures and refutations.} The loop's shape --- propose a law,
seek a counterexample, keep what survives --- is Lakatos's method reduced to
its mechanical shadow; Pease's computational model~\cite{pease2007lakatos}
works the same ground from the philosophy side.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
%% ============================================================================

One bench. The walker has closed one family of goals over one derived
operation on one toy algebra. Nothing here claims generality: the paper's
claim is that the gate --- close something chaining cannot --- has been
crossed with every step measured, and that two of those measurements
(replacement-not-addition; size-ranking selects noise) are design laws that
survive the bench. The schema list is open. The walker does not learn: it
retains nothing between runs, and the title of the editorial plan for this
paper --- ``learning to propose'' --- has deliberately \emph{not} been kept,
because the code does not yet earn it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ============================================================================

The companion paper's thesis was that the last mile is located, not crossed:
a search should fail by naming what it lacks. This paper kept the naming and
added the crossing --- and the crossing turned out to be made of the same
material as the locating. The walker closes $B_4$ not by searching harder but
by changing what it searches \emph{with}: mine the goal for its own
operation, refute the false laws for pennies, pay the kernel for the true
ones, and then --- the step the measurements insist on --- \emph{set the raw
laws aside}. Every number in this paper that surprised us is the same law
seen from a different side: knowledge that is not replaced is weight
(72.6\,s alone, 361\,s with one extra lemma, 962\,s cumulative, over 580\,s
with all six); and a walk that grows its pool without pruning fails by
growing, exactly like the raw searches of the companion paper.

What the walker still is not: a learner. It retains nothing between runs;
the learned-proposer organ that remembers which witnesses paid is wired to
the need organ, not yet to the walk. Connecting them --- so that the ladder's
first rung is ordered by remembered success rather than by MDL gain alone
--- is the natural next step, and this time the gate for claiming
``learning'' in a title is already written: the memory must change a
measured outcome on a goal the memoryless walker fails.
